Teaching

Proposed teaching contributions at the undergraduate and advanced (Int-PhD/Ph.D.) levels.

The main objectives of this process are to present accumulated knowledge on a given subject in a concise and organized form in order to prepare the students for continuing their studies with more advanced courses or for applying the acquired knowledge in their professional realization.

Teaching skills can be developed through experience and hard work, but the ability to teach is mostly a talent—a complex mixture of intellectual capacity, knowledge and understanding of the subject, and communicating skills—which cannot be taught themselves.

Teaching a course is also a learning process for the teacher, as interaction with the students provides an opportunity for better appreciation of the subject, stimulates one’s own work as a teacher and a scientist, and can be a very rewarding experience by itself.

Classical Thermodynamics

  • Systems, properties, changes and processes, heat and work.
  • Energy and the first law of thermodynamics, enthalpy and applications.
  • Exact differentials, Maxwell’s relations and the thermodynamic temperature scale.
  • Second law of thermodynamics, entropy and change in processes.
  • Equilibrium constant, Le Chatelier’s principle, chemical affinity and free energy.
  • Chemical potential, conductance, adsorption and surface tension.

Gas, Liquid and Solid State Physics

  • Kinetic theory of gases, real gases and intermolecular forces.
  • Characterization of the liquid state, surface tension, viscosity and colloids.
  • Macroscopic theory of dielectric dispersion, Debye and Onsager equations.
  • Characterization of solid states, unit cells, crystal systems and crystal planes.
  • X-ray diffraction, intensity and structure determination, techniques of crystal analysis.
